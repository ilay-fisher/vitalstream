# Tachycardia assessment

> SYNTHETIC EDUCATIONAL CONTENT for the VitalStream demo. Not real clinical guidance. Do not use for patient care.

## Definition

Tachycardia is a resting heart rate above 100 beats per minute. On NEWS2 a heart rate of 91 to 110 scores 1, 111 to 130 scores 2 and 131 or more scores 3 (a red score).

## First questions

Is the rhythm regular or irregular? Is the patient haemodynamically stable? Signs of instability are low blood pressure, chest pain, breathlessness, reduced consciousness and signs of shock. An unstable patient with tachycardia needs immediate senior or emergency help.

## Common causes

- Sinus tachycardia is the most frequent and is a response to pain, fever, anxiety, hypovolaemia, anaemia, hypoxaemia, sepsis or drugs. Treat the underlying cause rather than the heart rate.
- Atrial fibrillation or flutter: irregular rhythm, often rapid. Obtain a 12-lead ECG.
- Supraventricular tachycardia: abrupt onset, regular, usually above 150 beats per minute.
- Ventricular tachycardia: wide-complex rhythm, an emergency.

## Actions on the ward

1. Repeat the measurement manually and check the ECG trace for artefact.
2. Record a full set of observations and calculate NEWS2.
3. Obtain a 12-lead ECG for any new irregular rhythm or heart rate above 130.
4. Review fluid balance, bleeding, pain control, oxygen saturation and temperature.
5. Escalate according to the NEWS2 protocol; a heart rate of 131 or more is a red score and needs an urgent clinical review on its own.
