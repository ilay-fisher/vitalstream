# Hypotension management

> SYNTHETIC EDUCATIONAL CONTENT for the VitalStream demo. Not real clinical guidance. Do not use for patient care.

## Definition and NEWS2 scoring

Hypotension is commonly defined as a systolic blood pressure (SBP) below 90 mmHg or a mean arterial pressure below 65 mmHg. On NEWS2 an SBP of 101 to 110 scores 1, 91 to 100 scores 2 and 90 or less scores 3 (a red score). An SBP of 220 or more also scores 3.

## Assessment

Check the measurement first: cuff size, arm position and a repeat reading. Then assess perfusion: capillary refill, skin temperature, urine output, mental state and lactate. Review the trend; a drop of more than 40 mmHg from the patient's usual baseline matters even when the absolute value is not yet low.

## Common causes

Hypovolaemia from bleeding, dehydration or third-space losses; sepsis and vasodilation; cardiac causes such as myocardial infarction, arrhythmia or heart failure; drugs including antihypertensives, sedatives and opioids; adrenal insufficiency; anaphylaxis.

## Initial management

1. Lie the patient flat and, unless there is pulmonary oedema, raise the legs.
2. Secure intravenous access and give a fluid bolus of 250 to 500 ml of balanced crystalloid, reassessing after each bolus.
3. Give oxygen if saturation is low.
4. Stop or hold causative medicines after medical review.
5. Look for bleeding and infection; treat sepsis within the first hour if suspected.

## Escalation

Hypotension that does not respond to two fluid boluses, any SBP of 90 mmHg or lower with symptoms, or hypotension with tachycardia and fever requires immediate senior review and early critical care involvement for vasopressor support.
