# Fever management

> SYNTHETIC EDUCATIONAL CONTENT for the VitalStream demo. Not real clinical guidance. Do not use for patient care.

## Definition

Fever is a core temperature of 38.0 degrees Celsius or higher. On NEWS2, a temperature of 38.1 to 39.0 scores 1 and 39.1 or more scores 2; a temperature of 35.0 or below scores 3 (hypothermia is a red flag too). Hyperpyrexia refers to a temperature above 41.5 degrees Celsius and is an emergency.

## Find the source

Fever is a symptom, so look for the cause: chest, urine, wounds and surgical sites, intravenous lines, abdomen, skin, joints and drugs. Take blood cultures and send urine and sputum samples as indicated. Consider non-infectious causes such as venous thromboembolism, drug reactions, transfusion reaction and malignancy.

## Treatment

1. Treat comfort, not the number alone: paracetamol is the first-line antipyretic if there is distress.
2. Encourage oral fluids or give intravenous fluids if the patient is dehydrated.
3. Remove excess bedding; avoid cold sponging, which causes shivering.
4. Start antibiotics promptly when infection is probable; give them within one hour if sepsis is suspected.

## Escalation

A fever together with a heart rate above 90, respiratory rate above 20 or a falling blood pressure should trigger sepsis screening. Neutropenic patients with a fever are an emergency and need antibiotics immediately. Repeat observations at least hourly until the patient is stable.
