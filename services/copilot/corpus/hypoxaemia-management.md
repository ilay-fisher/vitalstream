# Hypoxaemia management

> SYNTHETIC EDUCATIONAL CONTENT for the VitalStream demo. Not real clinical guidance. Do not use for patient care.

## Definition and targets

Hypoxaemia is a low level of oxygen in the blood. In most adults the target oxygen saturation (SpO2) is 94 to 98 percent. For patients at risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure, such as those with severe COPD, the target is 88 to 92 percent and NEWS2 SpO2 scale 2 applies.

## Recognition

An SpO2 at or below 91 percent scores 3 on NEWS2 scale 1 and is a red score. Falling saturation often appears together with a rising respiratory rate and heart rate. Look for central cyanosis, use of accessory muscles and confusion.

## Immediate actions

1. Sit the patient upright and check the airway.
2. Verify the reading: check probe position, perfusion, nail polish and cold peripheries.
3. Start supplemental oxygen: nasal cannula for mild hypoxaemia, a reservoir mask at 10 to 15 litres per minute for severe hypoxaemia, titrating to the target range.
4. Call for urgent clinical review for any SpO2 below 92 percent, and for emergency help if saturation stays below 90 percent despite high-flow oxygen.
5. Check respiratory rate, work of breathing, auscultation and, if available, an arterial blood gas.

## Causes to consider

Pneumonia, pulmonary oedema, pulmonary embolism, pneumothorax, asthma or COPD exacerbation, aspiration and opioid-induced hypoventilation. Treat the cause as well as the number on the monitor.

## Escalation

Rising oxygen requirement over a short period is an early marker of deterioration. Escalate to the critical care team when the patient needs more than 40 percent inspired oxygen or shows exhaustion, drowsiness or a rising carbon dioxide level.
