# NEWS2 escalation protocol

> SYNTHETIC EDUCATIONAL CONTENT for the VitalStream demo. Not real clinical guidance. Do not use for patient care.

## Purpose

This protocol describes how a National Early Warning Score 2 (NEWS2) total maps to a monitoring frequency and an escalation response on a general ward. It is a decision-support aid and never replaces the judgement of the treating clinician.

## Scoring parameters

NEWS2 aggregates seven physiological parameters, each scored 0 to 3: respiratory rate, oxygen saturation (scale 1), supplemental oxygen (2 points if used), systolic blood pressure, heart rate, level of consciousness (ACVPU) and temperature. A single parameter scoring 3 is called a red score and is treated as a trigger in its own right even when the total is low.

## Escalation thresholds

- **Total 0 to 4 (low risk):** continue routine monitoring, minimum every 12 hours. A red score of 3 in any single parameter requires an urgent ward-based response: the registered nurse informs a clinician who decides whether to escalate care or increase the observation frequency.
- **Total 5 to 6 (medium risk):** urgent response. A clinician with core competencies in the care of acutely ill patients reviews the patient within one hour. Observations at least hourly. Consider whether critical care outreach input is needed.
- **Total 7 or more (high risk):** emergency response. Immediate review by a team with critical care competencies, usually the resuscitation or outreach team. Continuous monitoring of vital signs. Plan for transfer to a higher level of care.

## Practical notes

Recalculate the score after every complete set of observations and after any intervention such as oxygen, fluid or antibiotics. Document the score, the time, who was informed and the response. If the score rises by 2 or more points between two sets of observations, treat the patient as deteriorating and consider early senior review even if the total is still below a threshold.

## When the monitor and the patient disagree

Artefacts are common: a loose SpO2 probe, a movement-corrupted ECG or a cuff on the wrong arm. Check the patient first, then the equipment. Never dismiss a high score solely because a device may be faulty; repeat the measurement manually and escalate while doing so.
