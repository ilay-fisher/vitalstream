# Alarm fatigue policy

> SYNTHETIC EDUCATIONAL CONTENT for the VitalStream demo. Not real clinical guidance. Do not use for patient care.

## The problem

Staff exposed to a high number of non-actionable alarms become desensitised and may respond late or not at all to a genuine alarm. Studies of intensive care units report that a large share of monitor alarms are false or clinically irrelevant. Alarm fatigue is a patient safety hazard, not a nuisance.

## Principles

- Every alarm should be actionable. If an alarm does not change what a clinician does, adjust or remove it.
- Alarm limits are individualised to the patient and reviewed at every shift handover.
- Severity is tiered: critical alerts interrupt, medium alerts queue on the dashboard, low alerts are summarised.
- Duplicate alerts for the same patient, type and metric are suppressed for a short window (five minutes by default) so one deterioration raises one alert.

## Reducing noise

1. Skin preparation and electrode replacement every 24 hours to reduce ECG artefact.
2. Use delay times, so brief threshold crossings of a few seconds do not alarm.
3. Prefer trend-based and score-based alerts such as NEWS2 over single threshold alarms.
4. Review the top ten most frequent alarms weekly and act on the findings.
5. Never silence or disable an alarm permanently without a documented clinical decision.

## Responding

Acknowledge the alert in the system, attend the patient, and record the action taken. An acknowledged alert that is not resolved within the escalation window should be re-raised to the senior nurse. The copilot summarises alert history and trends but does not acknowledge alerts on anyone's behalf.
