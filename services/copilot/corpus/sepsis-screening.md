# Sepsis screening

> SYNTHETIC EDUCATIONAL CONTENT for the VitalStream demo. Not real clinical guidance. Do not use for patient care.

## Why screen

Sepsis is a dysregulated host response to infection that causes organ dysfunction. Early recognition and treatment within the first hour measurably improves survival, so continuous vital-sign monitoring is used to flag patients who might be developing it.

## Screening triggers

Consider sepsis when there is a suspected or confirmed infection together with two or more of the following:

- Temperature above 38.3 degrees Celsius or below 36.0 degrees Celsius
- Heart rate above 90 beats per minute (tachycardia)
- Respiratory rate above 20 breaths per minute (tachypnoea)
- Systolic blood pressure at or below 100 mmHg
- New confusion or reduced level of consciousness
- Oxygen saturation below 94 percent on room air

A NEWS2 total of 5 or more with a suspected infection should also trigger a sepsis review.

## First-hour actions

1. Obtain senior clinical review immediately.
2. Take blood cultures before antibiotics where this does not delay treatment.
3. Give broad-spectrum antibiotics within one hour of recognition for high-risk patients.
4. Start intravenous fluids and reassess perfusion after each bolus.
5. Measure serum lactate and urine output.
6. Give oxygen if saturation is low or the patient is breathless.

## Red-flag combinations

Hypotension with tachycardia and fever is the classic septic picture. A rising lactate, falling urine output or mottled skin signals septic shock and requires critical care escalation. Elderly and immunosuppressed patients may be afebrile, so rely on trends in heart rate, respiratory rate and mental state.
